# Insurance Market Stress Fact Sheet — Miami-Dade / Downtown Miami
Compiled 2026-08-20 for the "insurance market fragility" layer of the Miami flood-risk visualization. Every claim carries a URL citation. Screening-level compilation; verify against primary filings before publication.

## (a) Citizens Property Insurance — policies in force (PIF)

- **Statewide PIF, July 31, 2026: 278,196** (216,279 personal residential multiperil; 57,543 PR wind-only; remainder commercial). Source: Citizens policies-in-force page — https://www.citizensfla.com/policies-in-force
- **Statewide PIF, April 30, 2026: 294,894**; total exposure $88.5 billion. Source: https://www.citizensfla.com/-/20260430-policies-in-force
- **Statewide PIF, Dec 31, 2025: ~395,337–396,387** (lowest since at least 2012); officials projected ~385,000 by year-end. Sources: https://www.citizensfla.com/policies-in-force ; https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-12-27/citizens-property-insurance-now-has-fewer-than-400-000-policies
- **2023 peak: ~1.4 million policies** — i.e., a ~71% decline from peak to Dec 2025 and ~80% to mid-2026, driven by depopulation/takeout program and the 2022 litigation reforms. Source: https://www.wusf.org/politics-issues/2025-12-27/citizens-property-insurance-now-has-fewer-than-400-000-policies
- **Miami-Dade trend: Citizens PIF in Miami-Dade fell ~52% during 2025** (Broward −56%, Palm Beach −65%, Monroe −33%); the four Southeast Florida counties combined held ~144,000 Citizens policies at Dec 31, 2025 after shedding ~180,000 in the year (−56% regionally). Citizens' statewide market share fell below 10%. Source: https://www.axios.com/local/miami/2026/03/02/citizens-insurance-florida-miami-broward-palm-beach
- Rate context: Citizens announced 2026 average rate cuts (statewide avg −5.6% per the state; ~75% of Miami-Dade Citizens policyholders seeing decreases). Sources: https://www.citizensfla.com/-/20251210-citizens-recommends-rate-cuts-for-most-policyholders ; https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2025/governor-ron-desantis-announces-rate-reductions-miami-dade-county-auto-insurance
- *Gap:* an exact Miami-Dade-only Citizens policy count was not published in the pages fetched; Citizens posts county-level PIF spreadsheets at https://www.citizensfla.com/policies-in-force (monthly, by county) — pull the XLS for the precise figure.

## (c) Withdrawals / non-renewals since 2020 (still-solvent carriers pulling back)

- **Farmers Insurance** — announced July 2023 it would stop writing new Farmers-branded home/auto/umbrella policies in Florida, affecting roughly 100,000 policies (~30% of its FL book); subsidiaries Foremost and Bristol West continued. Sources: https://www.insurance.com/home-and-renters-insurance/home-insurers-leaving-florida ; https://fortune.com/2023/07/22/housing-market-florida-trouble-as-home-insurers-pull-out/
- **AAA (Auto Club)** — July 2023, non-renewed a "small percentage" of higher-exposure FL home/auto policies; did not exit the state. Sources: https://www.cbsnews.com/news/aaa-insurance-policies-florida-nonrenewal/ ; https://www.wtsp.com/article/news/regional/florida/aaa-insurance-dropping-florida-policies/67-afb2c7b7-7f31-4bbb-9378-6aa26c7bfc9b
- **Progressive** — reduced Florida property exposure/non-renewed portions of its home book. Source: https://www.insurance.com/home-and-renters-insurance/home-insurers-leaving-florida
- **Bankers Insurance** and **AIG's Lexington** — exited the Florida homeowners market. Source: https://www.insurance.com/home-and-renters-insurance/home-insurers-leaving-florida
- Overall: "more than a dozen insurers have left Florida or gone insolvent since 2020." Source: https://www.insurance.com/home-and-renters-insurance/home-insurers-leaving-florida
- Counter-trend (recovery signal): the state reports 11 new carriers entered the market 2023–2025. Source: https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2025/governor-ron-desantis-announces-rate-reductions-miami-dade-county-auto-insurance

## (d) NFIP Risk Rating 2.0 impact — Florida / Miami-Dade

- Florida (at RR2.0 rollout, Oct 2021): **~20% of the state's ~1.73 million NFIP policies got immediate decreases; ~80% face increases**, ~4% facing >$20/month increases. Florida had the most affected policies of any state. Source: https://www.valuepenguin.com/new-risk-rating-flood-insurance-rate-increases (analysis of FEMA state profile data; FEMA primary: https://www.fema.gov/sites/default/files/documents/fema_florida-state-profile_04-2025.pdf — blocked to automated fetch, verify manually)
- Miami-Dade (FEMA county data as summarized): **~347,092 NFIP policies affected; 22.2% (77,092) decreases; 77.8% increases**, of which 71.1% (246,303) are $0–10/month, 4% $10–20/mo, 2.4% $20–50/mo, 0.5% $50–100+/mo. Source: https://www.floodinsuranceguru.com/the-flood-insurance-guru-blog/florida-flood-insurance-miami-nfip-risk-rating-2.0-0
- Average flood premium: Florida ~**$600/yr** vs ~$1,000 national (pre-full-risk glide path); Miami-Dade average ~**$612/yr** vs $915 national. Under RR2.0, existing policies glide toward full risk at up to 18%/yr, so current premiums understate full-risk cost. Sources: https://www.floodinsuranceguru.com/the-flood-insurance-guru-blog/florida-flood-insurance-miami-nfip-risk-rating-2.0-0 ; https://www.fludzone.com/flood-zones/florida/miami ; RR2.0 mechanics: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IN11777

## (e) NFIP policies-in-force trend

- Florida: **~1.73 million policies (2021)** → **~1.8 million (Oct 2025)**; Florida holds far more NFIP policies than any state and 17.9% of FL homes have NFIP coverage (2nd highest share after Louisiana). Nationally PIF fell 0.2% Nov 2023–Nov 2024 while Florida held roughly firm. Sources: https://www.valuepenguin.com/new-risk-rating-flood-insurance-rate-increases ; https://www.cfpublic.org/housing-homelessness/2025-10-03/government-shutdown-hits-fema-flood-insurance-1-8-million-florida-homes ; https://www.valuepenguin.com/flood-insurance-policies-study
- Miami-Dade: **~410,136 active NFIP policies**, ~$250.9M annual premium; only ~1 in 3 households carries flood insurance; 62,027 claims totaling $855.6M since 1978. Source: https://www.fludzone.com/flood-zones/florida/miami
- Florida claims concentration: 51.5% of all 2023 NFIP claims and 67.7% of claim dollars were paid in Florida. Source: https://www.valuepenguin.com/flood-insurance-policies-study

## (f) FEMA Community Rating System — City of Miami

- **City of Miami: CRS Class 6 → 20% flood-insurance premium discount** for policies in the Special Flood Hazard Area (10% outside SFHA per standard CRS schedule). Source: https://www.fludzone.com/flood-zones/florida/miami (secondary; confirm class in FEMA's current CRS Eligible Communities list)
- Contrast: **unincorporated Miami-Dade County improved from Class 5 to Class 3 effective April 1, 2024 → 35% discount** (~100,000 policyholders), after ~$21M in flood-mitigation investment. Source: https://www.cbsnews.com/amp/miami/news/thousands-of-miami-dade-homeowners-will-soon-get-a-big-discount-on-flood-insurance ; county page: https://www.miamidade.gov/global/economy/building/flood-protection/insurance.page
- CRS mechanics (Class 1 = 45% discount ... Class 9 = 5%): https://www.fema.gov/floodplain-management/community-rating-system

## (g) Average homeowners (wind/all-peril) premium

- **Miami: ~$15,576/yr** (for $300K dwelling coverage, $1K deductible) vs **Florida ~$6,504** vs **national ~$2,844** (Insurify, 2026) — Miami ~2.4x the state average and ~5.5x the national average. Source: https://insurify.com/homeowners-insurance/florida/miami/
- State narrative: 2024 saw the lowest average homeowners premium increases in the nation (~1% statewide) per the Governor's office; treat as a state-sourced claim. Source: https://www.flgov.com/eog/news/press/2025/governor-ron-desantis-announces-rate-reductions-miami-dade-county-auto-insurance

## Interpretation for the "fragility" layer

Signals of stress concentrated in Miami-Dade: highest-in-nation homeowners premiums (~5.5x national), 10+ insolvencies since 2020, national carriers (Farmers/AAA/Progressive) pulling back, 78% of Miami-Dade NFIP policies on an upward full-risk glide path, and only ~1/3 flood take-up. Countervailing 2025–26 signals: Citizens depopulation to <300K policies, rate cuts, and new market entrants — i.e., fragility is easing on the wind side while flood-cost pressure (RR2.0) keeps rising.
